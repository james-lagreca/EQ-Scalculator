    Method notes

    Relations are solved in both directions. An inverse solution is an exact algebraic inversion of the stored forward relation rather than a separate regression — sound where the slope is theoretically fixed, but subject to regression dilution when inverting an ordinary least-squares fit.

    Relations may be piecewise: each is stored as ordered segments with validity ranges, so bilinear and trilinear models are represented natively. Inputs falling outside a model's published range are flagged as extrapolations. Validity ranges are published against the relation's own independent variable, so when a relation is solved inversely the range shown is mapped into the variable you are entering. Note that a published range states the authors' claimed applicability, which is not the same as the extent of the data they fitted: a relation may publish no upper bound and still have no observations anywhere near your input. Monte Carlo sampling perturbs the regression intercept, or applies the published scatter in log₁₀(Y), depending on which the source paper reports.
